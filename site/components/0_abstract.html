Abstract

Standard scintillation theory treats each scattered ray as carrying a scalar delay, so differential delays around a closed triplet cancel identically: \(\tau_{ij}+\tau_{jk}+\tau_{ki}=0\). The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) instead permits path-dependent proper-time transport in low-density, unscreened environments, producing a non-zero synchronization holonomy. This paper tests that null prediction in pulsar scintillation using the phase-domain closure statistic \(\psi\), a zero-centered circular observable designed to avoid the folded-noise bias that affects unsigned delay amplitudes.

The primary target is PSR J0437-4715, analyzed with 19,167 scintillation triplets from 1,391 closure-capable Parkes/PPTA epochs; 1,093 epochs form the independent sample. The broader 15-pulsar catalog includes PSR J1603-7202, ten Jiamusi pulsars, and three MeerKAT pulsars. Phase-domain weight for rejecting the \(\psi=0\) null is concentrated on J0437-4715; J1603 contributes bipolar geometric evidence from a distinct line of sight at complementary velocity geometry, while the Jiamusi and MeerKAT rows supply noise-limited environmental bounding checks consistent with TEP's predicted suppression in dense, distant environments.

J0437 shows a robust non-zero Phase Closure signal. The unweighted phase closure is \(\psi=+1.120\) rad with Rayleigh \(p=1.34\times10^{-44}\), identical in heliocentric and CMB-frame analyses. The weighted circular mean is \(\bar{\psi}=0.984\pm0.046\) rad with \(R_{\rm bar}=0.308\), V-test \(p=2.04\times10^{-5}\), and a 95% bootstrap confidence interval \([0.740,1.231]\) rad that excludes zero. This rejects the additive scalar path-delay null under the present pipeline.

J1603 has a 73.8° proper-motion separation from J0437 and exhibits a frame-independent bipolar geometric structure that TEP predicts when the monopole is washed out by high dispersion. The Jiamusi and MeerKAT samples are noise-limited, consistent with TEP's predicted environmental suppression in dense, distant environments. The raw unsigned delay magnitude for J0437, \(|H|=8.100\pm0.102\) ns, is saturated by the folded-normal noise floor \(E[|H|]=6.810\pm0.102\) ns, as expected for folded magnitudes. The robust trimmed amplitude \(H_{\rm trim}=21.991\pm0.483\) ns (45.5σ) removes heavy-tail ISM outliers and provides a primary delay-domain measurement of the holonomy amplitude.

Several checks strengthen the phase-domain result. Phase-scramble and pre-alignment controls pass, unweighted \(\psi\) is frame-invariant, signed-delay cancellation behaves as expected for a bipolar signal, and synthetic/noise tests show no pure-noise false positive in the tested configuration. A signed-delay orbital diagnostic shows coherent triplet-level structure; a hierarchical mixed-effects model (amplitude 1.14 ± 0.79 ns, likelihood-ratio \(p = 0.357\)) and an epoch-blocked sinusoid (\(p = 0.449\), phase-permutation control \(p = 0.513\)) quantify the partially screened orbital channel expected when the white dwarf companion attenuates orbital-scale Temporal Shear. Multi-pulsar scaling, chromaticity, cross-telescope environmental bounds, and orbital kinematics are therefore read as directional consistency checks that sharpen alongside larger phase-detected sightline samples.

The principal empirical result is non-zero Phase Closure on the J0437 sightline, consistent with synchronization holonomy in the TEP framework as the best-fitting organized interpretation of the phase-domain evidence under the present pipeline. Within the enumerated scalar-delay ISM models implemented in Section 4.2 (thin-screen Kolmogorov, multi-screen, refractive wandering, chromatic plasma, Doppler-delay covariance, and telescope calibration phase), each predicts \(\psi = 0\) and is rejected by the J0437 phase data under the stated assumptions. Independent geometric evidence from J1603 and environmental-suppression signatures in the distant sample already broaden the case beyond a bare multi-channel evidence (phase gate, geometry, environmental bounds); extending the phase-detected census refines numerical scaling laws under the same pipeline.
