Appendix A. Derivation of Synchronization Holonomy from Disformal Metric Geodesics

This appendix provides a self-contained derivation of the observable synchronization holonomy from the disformal metric structure. The derivation proceeds from the geodesic equation in the matter metric, showing explicitly that non-integrability of the proper-time 1-form leads to a non-zero closure when \(B(\phi) \neq 0\).

A.1 Disformal metric structure

TEP posits that all non-gravitational fields couple to the matter metric \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\), related to the gravitational metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\) by the disformal map (Bekenstein 1993; Koivisto & Zumalacárregui 2012):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_disformal} \tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)\,g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\,\nabla_{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi . \end{equation}

In the weak-field, static limit the gravitational metric is \(g_{00}=-(1+2\Phi)\), \(g_{ij}=(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}\) with \(|\Phi|\ll 1\). Assuming the scalar field is time-independent (\(\nabla_0\phi=0\)), the matter-metric components are

\begin{align} \tilde{g}_{00} &= -A^2(\phi)\,(1+2\Phi), \\ \tilde{g}_{ij} &= A^2(\phi)\,(1-2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij} + B(\phi)\,\partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi . \end{align}

A.2 Proper-time 1-form and its exterior derivative

The matter-metric proper-time 1-form is \(\tilde{\tau} = \tilde{g}_{0\mu} dx^\mu\). In the static limit with \(\tilde{g}_{0i}=0\), this reduces to \(\tilde{\tau} = \tilde{g}_{00} dt = -A^2(\phi)(1+2\Phi) dt\). The coordinate-time differential along a spatial path element \(dl\) with tangent \(\hat{n}^i = dx^i/dl\) follows from the null condition \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu = 0\):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_dt} dt = \frac{1}{c}\sqrt{\frac{-\tilde{g}_{ij}\,dx^i dx^j}{-\tilde{g}_{00}}} = \frac{dl}{c}\sqrt{\frac{1-2\Phi}{1+2\Phi} + \frac{B(\phi)}{A^2(\phi)(1+2\Phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2 } . \end{equation}

To leading order in the perturbations,

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_dt_approx} dt \approx \frac{dl}{c}\Bigl[\,1 - 2\Phi + \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2\Bigr]. \end{equation}

The proper-time 1-form along the path is therefore \(d\tilde{\tau} = \tilde{g}_{00} dt \approx -A^2(\phi)[1 - 2\Phi + \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2] dl\). The exterior derivative is

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_exterior} d(d\tilde{\tau}) \propto \nabla\left[\frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2\right] \times d\mathbf{l}, \end{equation}

which is non-zero when both \(B(\phi)\) and \(\nabla\phi\) vary along the path. This non-vanishing exterior derivative is the origin of the holonomy: the 1-form \(d\tilde{\tau}\) is non-integrable, so its line integral around a closed loop depends on the path geometry rather than being identically zero.

A.3 Closure holonomy from Stokes' theorem

Consider a closed scattering loop \(C\) formed by three arclets. By Stokes' theorem, the synchronization holonomy is

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_stokes} H = \oint_C d\tilde{\tau} = \int_S d(d\tilde{\tau}), \end{equation}

where \(S\) is any surface bounded by \(C\). The integrand \(d(d\tilde{\tau})\) is proportional to \(\nabla B(\phi) \times \nabla\phi\) in the eikonal approximation. For a thin scattering screen where \(\nabla\phi\) is approximately constant across the loop, the surface integral reduces to

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_holonomy} H = \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}\,(\nabla\phi)^2\,\oint_C (\hat{n}\cdot\hat{e}_\phi)^2\,dl , \end{equation}

where \(\hat{e}_\phi = \nabla\phi/|\nabla\phi|\). The loop integral is a positive geometric factor \(L_C\) with dimensions of length, yielding

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_holonomy_final} H = \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}\,(\nabla\phi)^2\,L_C . \end{equation}

Equation \ref{eq:app_holonomy_final} is the central result: the closure holonomy vanishes if and only if the disformal coupling is zero, \(B(\phi)=0\). In purely conformal theories (\(B=0\)) the exterior derivative \(d(d\tilde{\tau}) = 0\) identically, so the holonomy vanishes by Stokes' theorem, recovering the standard additive-delay null hypothesis. A non-zero holonomy at leading order is therefore a direct, unavoidable consequence of \(B(\phi)\neq 0\).

A.4 Mapping to the measured closure observables

The delay-domain observable is the closure delay \(\tau_{\text{closure}}\), which is proportional to \(H\). Because the scintillation loop carries a natural orientation in the \((\tau, f_D)\) plane, the delay and Doppler closures define a complex number

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_complex} Z = \tau_{\text{closure}} + i\,f_{D,\text{closure}} . \end{equation}

The Phase Closure is \(\psi = \arg(Z)\). Under the additive-delay null hypothesis both \(\tau_{\text{closure}}\) and \(f_{D,\text{closure}}\) are zero (up to measurement noise), so \(\psi\) is uniformly distributed. When \(B(\phi)\neq 0\), Equation \ref{eq:app_holonomy_final} imprints a non-zero mean on both the real and imaginary parts of \(Z\) with a ratio fixed by the geometric orientation of the triplet relative to the effective velocity vector. The angular distribution of \(\psi\) therefore clusters around a non-zero mean, providing the circular statistic used as the primary detection test in this work.
