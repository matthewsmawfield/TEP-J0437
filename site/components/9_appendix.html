Appendix A. Derivation of Synchronization Holonomy from Disformal Metric Geodesics

This appendix provides a self-contained derivation of the observable synchronization holonomy from the disformal metric structure. The derivation proceeds from the geodesic equation in the matter metric, showing explicitly that non-integrability of the synchronization 1-form leads to a non-zero closure when \(B(\phi) \neq 0\).

A.1 Disformal metric structure

TEP posits that all non-gravitational fields couple to the matter metric \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\), related to the gravitational metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\) by the disformal map (Bekenstein 1993; Koivisto & Zumalacárregui 2012):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_disformal} \tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)\,g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\,\nabla_{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi . \end{equation}

In the weak-field, static limit the gravitational metric is \(g_{00}=-(1+2\Phi)\), \(g_{ij}=(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}\) with \(|\Phi|\ll 1\). Assuming the scalar field is time-independent (\(\nabla_0\phi=0\)) at fixed cosmic epoch (\(A_{\rm sp}(t_0)=1\)), the canonical matter-metric components are \(\tilde g_{00}=-A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav})(1+2\Phi)\) and \(\tilde g_{ij}=(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}+B(\phi)\partial_i\phi\partial_j\phi\) (or in the isotropic-conformal shorthand,

\begin{align} \tilde{g}_{00} &= -A^2(\phi)\,(1+2\Phi), \\ \tilde{g}_{ij} &= A^2(\phi)\,(1-2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij} + B(\phi)\,\partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi , \end{align}

both sharing the identical leading projected disformal synchronization connection \(\delta\tilde\sigma_i\) below).

A.2 Residual synchronization connection and projected disformal correction

Following the Jakarta construction (Paper 0, Section 3.3 and Appendix A3), the physical observable is not the raw loop integral of a proper-time 1-form but the GR-subtracted residual. Let \(\tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)}\) denote the matter-frame synchronization transport one-form and \(\sigma_{\rm GR}^{(\tau)}\) the corresponding GR reference one-form. The residual connection is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_residual} \Delta\sigma^{(\tau)} = \tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)} - \sigma_{\rm GR}^{(\tau)} \end{equation}

and the time-valued residual holonomy is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_holonomy_resid} H_{\rm resid}^{(\tau)}(C) = \oint_C \Delta\sigma^{(\tau)} = \iint_\Sigma d(\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}), \qquad C = \partial\Sigma. \end{equation}

This is invariant under synchronization re-gaugings because both \(\tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)}\) and \(\sigma_{\rm GR}^{(\tau)}\) shift by the same exact one-form. In the purely conformal subclass (\(B = 0\)), the scalar contribution is the exact one-form \(\omega^{(A)} = d\ln A\), so \(\oint_C d\ln A = 0\) for any closed loop. A leading-order nonzero \(H_{\rm resid}^{(\tau)}\) requires the disformal sector.

To leading order in the disformal correction, let \(u^\mu\) denote the four-velocity of the clock congruence and \(P_\mu{}^\nu = \delta_\mu{}^\nu + u_\mu u^\nu\) the spatial projector. The projected disformal contribution to the synchronization connection is (Jakarta, Appendix A3):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_disformal_sigma} \delta\tilde{\sigma}_\mu^{(\tau)} \simeq -\frac{B}{A^2}\,(u \cdot \nabla\phi)\,P_\mu{}^\nu\,\nabla_\nu\phi \end{equation}

In a hypersurface-orthogonal \(3+1\) slicing (\(u^\mu = n^\mu\)):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_3plus1} \delta\tilde{\sigma}_i^{(\tau)} \simeq -\frac{B}{A^2 N}\,(\partial_i \phi)\,(n \cdot \partial\phi) \end{equation}

Unlike \(d\ln A\), the disformal contribution is not generically exact. Its curvature satisfies \(d(\delta\tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)}) \neq 0\) whenever the prefactor varies independently around the loop — through time dependence, motion through \(\nabla\phi\), anisotropic boundary conditions, inhomogeneous field structure, or spatial variation of the disformal response.

A.3 Closure holonomy and the physical condition for non-vanishing signal

At leading order beyond the GR-subtracted reference model:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_dsigma} d(\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}) = d(\tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)} - \sigma_{\rm GR}^{(\tau)}) \supset d(\delta\tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)}) \end{equation}

A static scalar gradient in a hypersurface-orthogonal frame does not generate the leading holonomy. The pulsar observable requires \(u \cdot \nabla\phi \neq 0\): relative motion through the field, time dependence, mixed temporal-spatial structure, or independently varying lapse/shift and disformal response around the effective loop. For J0437−4715, the pulsar's proper motion (\(v_\perp \simeq 104\) km/s), Earth's orbital motion (\(v_{\rm orb} \simeq 30\) km/s), and screen motion provide the kinematic ingredients that generate \(u \cdot \nabla\phi \neq 0\). These enter the theoretical prediction through the \(u \cdot \nabla\phi\) factor in equation $\eqref{eq:app_disformal_sigma}$, not merely through the later Stokes-alignment algorithm.

The leading-order residual holonomy for a loop \(C\) bounding a smooth surface \(\Sigma\) is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_holonomy_final} H_{\rm resid}^{(\tau)}(C) = \iint_\Sigma d(\delta\tilde{\sigma}^{(\tau)}) \end{equation}

Equation $\ref{eq:app_holonomy_final}$ is the central result: the residual holonomy vanishes in the conformal-only subclass (\(B = 0\)), where the scalar contribution is exact. A non-zero \(H_{\rm resid}^{(\tau)}\) at leading order is possible when \(B(\phi) \neq 0\) and \(u \cdot \nabla\phi \neq 0\) combine to produce nonzero residual curvature. The precise necessary condition is:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_curvature_condition} d\!\left[\frac{B}{A^2}\,(u \cdot \nabla\phi)\,P \cdot \nabla\phi\right] \neq 0 \end{equation}

over the loop region. This is stronger than merely requiring \(B \neq 0\) and \(u \cdot \nabla\phi \neq 0\) individually, because it specifies exactly when the curvature of the residual connection — and therefore the holonomy — is nonzero. The conformal sector alone cannot generate a closed-loop residual; it can affect local rates and open-path redshift comparisons but not the synchronization holonomy.

A.4 Why ordinary ray phases close but TEP synchronization transport need not

A central question is whether the measured secondary-spectrum cross-term phases are the edges of a genuine spacetime synchronization loop, or merely differences between three complete source-observer ray phases that would trivially cancel. The following measurement model identifies the observed cross-terms with synchronization-transport links. A complete electromagnetic transfer derivation is required to determine the corresponding channel coefficient and effective phase frequency \(\omega_{\rm eff}\); the identification below is a measurement-model postulate, not a derivation from the matter-coupled electromagnetic field.

For three single-ray complex amplitudes \(X_i, X_j, X_k\) at the observer, rank-one pairwise cross-terms \(Q_{ab} = X_a X_b^*\) obey the algebraic identity \(Q_{ij}Q_{jk}Q_{ki} = |X_i X_j X_k|^2\), whose phase vanishes identically regardless of the scalar phase \(\Theta_a\) accumulated along each individual ray: \((\Theta_i - \Theta_j) + (\Theta_j - \Theta_k) + (\Theta_k - \Theta_i) = 0\). In the secondary-spectrum measurement pipeline, however, each extracted arclet peak \(Q_{ab}(\tau_{ab}, f_{D,ab})\) is not a point-wise product of isolated single-ray amplitudes; it is the two-dimensional Fourier transform of the dynamic intensity spectrum \(I(\nu,t) = |E(\nu,t)|^2\) evaluated over a finite delay–Doppler pixel bin integrating a coherent bundle of sub-rays across the scattering screen (as demonstrated explicitly by the non-factorizable \(\chi^2\) screen simulation of Step 055, where secondary-spectrum cross-terms are intensity convolutions that break rank-one factorization). While conventional multi-ray screen convolutions produce non-zero per-epoch closure phases whose epoch-mean direction averages to zero over time (Step 055: synthetic \(\bar{R}_{\rm dir}=0.09\), Rayleigh \(p=0.55\)), a stationary non-exact spacetime synchronization connection \(\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\) couples to the finite-baseline interference transport across each screen-pair link \(\Gamma_{ab}\), breaking rank-one factorization with a persistent directional bias.

TEP modifies the operational comparison itself. The pairwise observable is not merely the difference of endpoint phases, but the transport of synchronization between the path-defined events associated with rays \(a\) and \(b\):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tep_transport} \varphi_{ab}^{\rm TEP} = \varphi_b - \varphi_a + \int_{\Gamma_{ab}} \omega_{\rm eff}(x)\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)} \end{equation}

where \(\Gamma_{ab}\) is the comparison path in spacetime induced by the scattering geometry: the spatial separation between the points where rays \(a\) and \(b\) intersect the scattering screen, transported along the observer's worldline. The endpoint phases \(\varphi_a, \varphi_b\) are state functions and cancel in the closure; the residual synchronization transport \(\int_{\Gamma_{ab}} \omega_{\rm eff}\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\) is path-dependent and generically non-exact when \(B(\phi) \neq 0\).

The three measured cross-terms \((ij), (jk), (ki)\) correspond to three comparison paths \(\Gamma_{ij}, \Gamma_{jk}, \Gamma_{ki}\) that form a genuine closed spacetime loop:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:closure_loop} \psi_{ijk} = \varphi_{ij}^{\rm TEP} + \varphi_{jk}^{\rm TEP} + \varphi_{ki}^{\rm TEP} = \operatorname{Arg}\!\exp\!\left[i\oint_C \omega_{\rm eff}(x)\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\right] = \operatorname{Arg}\!\exp\!\left[i\iint_\Sigma d(\omega_{\rm eff}\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)})\right] \end{equation}

The endpoint phases cancel telescopically, while the residual synchronization curvature remains. Thus the null \(\psi = 0\) is recovered whenever \(\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\) is exact — including the conformal-only sector — whereas the disformal non-exact sector permits \(\psi \neq 0\). The measured cross-term is non-factorizable under TEP precisely because the synchronization transport along each edge is an independent path integral, not a difference of state functions.

In the scintillation geometry, each arclet \(i\) in the secondary spectrum corresponds to a discrete scattered ray arriving at the observer with delay \(\tau_i\) and Doppler shift \(f_{D,i}\). The physical events and paths are:

  • \(S_i, S_j, S_k\): the three scattering-screen events where rays \(i, j, k\) are redirected by plasma inhomogeneities.
  • \(O_i, O_j, O_k\): the corresponding observer detection events (co-located at the receiver, but distinguished by their arrival directions and delays in the secondary spectrum).
  • \(u^\mu\): the physical clock congruence (the observer's four-velocity, including Earth orbital motion and station velocity).
  • \(\Gamma_{ij}\): the oriented synchronization-comparison link between the scattering events \(S_i\) and \(S_j\), transported along the observer's worldline.
  • \(\Gamma_{jk}, \Gamma_{ki}\): the corresponding links for the other two pairs.
  • \(C = \Gamma_{ij} + \Gamma_{jk} + \Gamma_{ki}\): the closed synchronization-comparison loop.

The cross-term \(\hat{\tau}_{ab}\) is measured from the interference of rays \(a\) and \(b\) in the secondary spectrum, and its phase \(\phi_{ab}\) is extracted from the complex cross-spectrum. The three cross-terms \((i,j), (j,k), (k,i)\) are measured independently from three distinct pairs of arclets, and their closure \(\phi_{ij} + \phi_{jk} - \phi_{ik}\) corresponds to the loop \(C = \Gamma_{ij} + \Gamma_{jk} + \Gamma_{ki}\) in the scattering geometry. The minus sign on \(\phi_{ik}\) reflects the reversal of the \((i,k)\) leg to close the loop. Under the scalar-delay null, each \(\phi_{ab}\) is a state-function difference and the closure vanishes; under TEP, each \(\phi_{ab}\) carries an additional path-dependent synchronization transport that does not cancel. The measured complex cross-term acquires \(\int_{\Gamma_{ab}} \omega_{\rm eff}\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\) through the electromagnetic phase transport between the scattering events, calibrated by the emitter and receiver clock congruence; photon propagation is null, so the phase is formulated through clock calibration and electromagnetic phase transport, not photon proper time.

Amplitude closure of the measurement model is evaluated in Step 057 (Section 5.4.3): the canonical connection integrated over the measured screen-triangle geometry on the admissible \(B \geq 0\) branch yields \(\approx 1.47\times10^{-12}\) s at the \(B_0=+1\) unit benchmark (\(1.3\%\) of \(H_{\rm implied}\approx 1.135\times10^{-10}\) s on the median leg, \(5.0\%\) on the \(p_{95}\) leg) and reaches \(1.141\times10^{-10}\) s (\(100.5\%\) on the median leg; \(B_0^{\rm req}=20.1\) on the \(p_{95}\) leg) at the corpus's GW170817 galactic-profile bound \(B_0\lesssim 77.7\) (Paper 0, Step 50), while remaining below the direct time-domain delay bound \(|H|\le 0.459\) ns (Section 4.4).

A.5 Mapping to the measured closure observables

The measured Phase Closure is the complex cross-term closure product \(\mathcal{B}_{ijk} = Q_{ij} Q_{jk} Q_{ik}^*\) (Equation $\ref{eq:closure_product}$), where each \(Q_{ab}\) is extracted from the complex secondary spectrum at the cross-term peak position. The TEP measurement operator modifies each cross-term:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tep_cross_term} Q_{ab}^{\rm TEP} = X_a\,X_b^*\,\exp\!\left[i\int_{\Gamma_{ab}} \omega_{\rm eff}(x)\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\right] . \end{equation}

Within the TEP measurement model, the measured cross-term is represented as above. Appendix A.4 defines the associated events and paths; electromagnetic transfer determines the channel coefficient. The closure product is then:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:tep_closure_product} Q_{ij}^{\rm TEP}\,Q_{jk}^{\rm TEP}\,Q_{ki}^{\rm TEP} = |X_i X_j X_k|^2\,\exp\!\left[i\oint_C \omega_{\rm eff}(x)\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\right] , \end{equation}

and therefore:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:psi_holonomy} \psi_{ijk} = \operatorname{Arg}\!\exp\!\left[i\oint_C \omega_{\rm eff}(x)\,\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)}\right] . \end{equation}

The endpoint amplitudes and phases cancel, leaving only the residual synchronization holonomy converted to phase through \(\omega_{\rm eff}\). Under the additive-delay null hypothesis (\(\Delta\sigma^{(\tau)} = 0\)), \(\psi_{ijk} = 0\) up to measurement noise. When \(B(\phi)\neq 0\) and the curvature condition (Equation $\ref{eq:app_curvature_condition}$) is satisfied, the nonfactorizable synchronization transport imprints a non-zero mean on \(\psi\), providing the circular statistic used as the primary detection test in this work. The delay-domain folded magnitude \(M_{\rm cl}\) is a separate quantity, computed from the real-valued closure delay \(\tau_{\text{closure}}\); it is not the real part of a complex delay-Doppler vector and is not used to define \(\psi\).

Appendix B. Limitations of Delay-Domain Amplitude Estimators

While the Phase Closure \(\psi\) is a zero-centered odd-parity circular statistic, unsigned delay-domain amplitude estimators of the form \(M_{\rm cl} = |\langle \tau_{\text{closure}} \rangle_{\text{aligned}}|\) are strictly non-negative and therefore subject to folded-normal (Rice-distribution) noise-floor bias. For zero-mean Gaussian noise with standard deviation \(\sigma\), the expectation of the folded magnitude is \(E[|X|] = \sigma\sqrt{2/\pi} > 0\). Consequently, raw unsigned delay magnitudes and information-criterion comparisons evaluated on \(M_{\rm cl}\) against a zero-mean null primarily measure the non-zero expectation of the folded noise distribution rather than a physical synchronization holonomy. This appendix documents the complete delay-domain amplitude audit from Step 007, Step 052, and Step 060 for pipeline completeness.

B.1 Raw, Noise-Subtracted, and Symmetrically Trimmed Delay Estimators

In the primary PSR J0437-4715 dataset (1,093 independent epochs), the inverse-variance-weighted epoch-level unsigned closure magnitude is \(M_{\rm cl} = 8.100 \pm 0.26\) ns (epoch-level bootstrap SEM; the internal measurement-noise SEM is \(0.102\) ns). Estimating the per-epoch folded-normal noise expectation from the within-epoch signed-delay dispersion yields \(E[M_{\rm cl}] = 6.810 \pm 0.24\) ns, demonstrating that the raw unsigned estimator is dominated by the Rice noise floor. Subtracting the per-epoch floor in paired epoch-level comparisons leaves a statistically positive residual delay excess of \(M_{\rm cl,excess} = 1.290 \pm 0.11\) ns (\(t \approx 12.2\sigma\) under independent-epoch resampling, and \(3.8\sigma\)–\(10.8\sigma\) across 15–240-epoch MJD-ordered contiguous block bootstraps; Steps 052 and 060).

A secondary delay-domain diagnostic, the symmetric 10% trimmed amplitude \(M_{\rm cl,sym10} = 21.991 \pm 0.75\) ns (epoch-level iid bootstrap SEM, rising to \(\pm 2.0\text{--}4.9\) ns under 15–240-epoch contiguous blocks; Step 052), removes the lowest and highest 10% of unweighted per-epoch folded magnitudes. Because \(M_{\rm cl,sym10}\) is an unweighted trimmed summary of per-epoch folded magnitudes whereas \(M_{\rm cl} = 8.100\) ns is an inverse-variance-weighted global mean, their numerical scales reflect their distinct weighting conventions rather than two estimates of the same parameter, and neither carries primary inferential weight.
