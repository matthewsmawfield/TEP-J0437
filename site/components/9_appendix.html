Appendix A. Derivation of Synchronization Holonomy from Disformal Coupling

This appendix provides a self-contained derivation of the observable synchronization holonomy from the two-metric scalar-tensor structure defined in Section 1. No result from external theoretical works is required.

A.1 Propagation metric for non-gravitational fields

TEP posits that all non-gravitational fields couple to the matter metric \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\), related to the gravitational metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\) by the disformal map

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_disformal} \tilde{g}_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)\,g_{\mu\nu} + B(\phi)\,\nabla_{\mu}\phi\,\nabla_{\nu}\phi . \end{equation}

In the weak-field, static limit the gravitational metric is written as \(g_{00}=-(1+2\Phi)\), \(g_{ij}=(1-2\Phi)\delta_{ij}\) with \(|\Phi|\ll 1\), and assume the scalar field \(\phi\) is time-independent so that \(\nabla_{0}\phi=0\). The matter-metric components are then

\begin{align} \tilde{g}_{00} &= -A^2(\phi)\,(1+2\Phi), \\ \tilde{g}_{ij} &= A^2(\phi)\,(1-2\Phi)\,\delta_{ij} + B(\phi)\,\partial_i\phi\,\partial_j\phi . \end{align}

A.2 Coordinate time delay along a scattered path

For a photon, the null condition on the matter metric reads \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu = 0\). In the static geometry this gives the coordinate time increment along a spatial path element \(dl\) with unit tangent \(\hat{n}^i = dx^i/dl\):

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_dt} dt = \frac{1}{c}\sqrt{\frac{-\tilde{g}_{ij}\,dx^i dx^j}{-\tilde{g}_{00}}} = \frac{dl}{c}\sqrt{\frac{1-2\Phi}{1+2\Phi} + \frac{B(\phi)}{A^2(\phi)(1+2\Phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2 } . \end{equation}

To leading order in \(\Phi\) and the disformal perturbation,

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_dt_approx} dt \approx \frac{dl}{c}\Bigl[\,1 - 2\Phi + \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2\Bigr]. \end{equation}

The term \(-2\Phi\) is the usual gravitational potential; it is a scalar function of position and therefore yields a vanishing line integral around any closed loop once common-mode contributions are removed. The second term, proportional to \(B(\phi)\), depends on the projection of the spatial gradient \(\nabla\phi\) onto the path direction \(\hat{n}\). It is not the gradient of any scalar potential.

A.3 Closure holonomy

Consider a closed scattering loop \(C\) formed by three arclets. The physical coordinate time accumulated around the loop is

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_closure_time} \Delta t_{\rm closure} = \oint_C dt = \frac{1}{c}\oint_C \Bigl[\frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}(\nabla\phi\cdot\hat{n})^2\Bigr]\,dl , \end{equation}

where the \(-2\Phi\) term has dropped out because it is exact. The remaining integrand is a path-dependent scalar. In the geometric-optics limit appropriate for interstellar scintillation, the loop is confined to a thin plasma screen and the gradient \(\nabla\phi\) is approximately constant across the loop. Parameterizing the loop by its spatial coordinate and writing \(\hat{e}_\phi = \nabla\phi/|\nabla\phi|\), the integral reduces to

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_holonomy} H \equiv c\,\Delta t_{\rm closure} = \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}\,(\nabla\phi)^2\,\oint_C (\hat{n}\cdot\hat{e}_\phi)^2\,dl . \end{equation}

For a non-degenerate loop the integral is a positive geometric factor \(L_C\) with dimensions of length. Therefore

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_holonomy_final} H = \frac{B(\phi)}{2A^2(\phi)}\,(\nabla\phi)^2\,L_C . \end{equation}

Equation \ref{eq:app_holonomy_final} is the central result: the closure holonomy vanishes if and only if the disformal coupling is zero, \(B(\phi)=0\). In purely conformal theories (\(B=0\)) the loop integral is identically zero, recovering the standard additive-delay null hypothesis. A non-zero holonomy at leading order is therefore a direct, unavoidable consequence of \(B(\phi)\neq 0\).

A.4 Mapping to the measured closure observables

Section 1 writes synchronization holonomy as \(H \equiv \oint_C d\tilde{\tau}\), the loop integral of matter-metric proper time. The coordinate-time derivation above expresses the same closure content for photons on \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\): the holonomy variable \(H\) in Equation \ref{eq:app_holonomy_final} is \(c\) times the net coordinate delay accumulated around the loop after the piece driven by the single-valued Newtonian potential \(\Phi\) drops from the closure sum, as in Equation \ref{eq:app_closure_time}. Section 2 defines the measured closure delay \(\tau_{\text{closure}}\) (Equation \ref{eq:closure_delay}) and the Stokes-aligned aggregate used for \(|H|\) (Equation \ref{eq:holonomy_magnitude}). The complex closure \(Z=\tau_{\text{closure}}+i f_{D,\text{closure}}\) and \(\psi=\arg(Z)\) are fixed operationally in Section 2.1. Appendix A motivates why \(B(\phi)\neq 0\) forbids an additive-delay representation in which both \(\tau_{\text{closure}}\) and \(f_{D,\text{closure}}\) vanish up to noise, biasing \(\psi\) away from a uniform circular null.

A.5 From holonomy to the Phase Closure \(\psi\)

The delay-domain observable is the closure delay \(\tau_{\rm closure}\), which is proportional to \(H\). Because the scintillation loop carries a natural orientation in the \((\tau,f_D)\) plane, the delay and Doppler closures define a complex number

\begin{equation} \label{eq:app_complex} Z = \tau_{\rm closure} + i\,f_{D,\rm closure} . \end{equation}

The Phase Closure is \(\psi = \arg(Z)\). Under the additive-delay null hypothesis both \(\tau_{\rm closure}\) and \(f_{D,\rm closure}\) are zero (up to measurement noise), so \(\psi\) is uniformly distributed. When \(B(\phi)\neq 0\), Equation \ref{eq:app_holonomy_final} imprints a non-zero mean on both the real and imaginary parts of \(Z\) with a ratio fixed by the geometric orientation of the triplet relative to the effective velocity vector. The angular distribution of \(\psi\) therefore clusters around a non-zero mean, providing the circular statistic used as the primary detection test in this work.
