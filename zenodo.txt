The scalar-factorizable thin-screen delay model tested here assigns each scattered ray an additive scalar delay, so differential delays around a closed triplet cancel identically: τ_ij+τ_jk+τₖᵢ=0. The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) instead predicts that proper-time transport is path-dependent in dilute, unscreened environments, producing a non-zero synchronization holonomy. This paper reports the rejection of the scalar-delay null hypothesis in pulsar scintillation using the phase-domain closure statistic ψ, a zero-centered circular observable that separates geometric phase from folded-noise bias.

The primary target is PSR J0437-4715, analyzed with 19,167 scintillation triplets from 1,391 closure-capable Parkes/PPTA epochs; 1,093 epochs form the independent sample. The broader 15-pulsar catalog includes PSR J1603-7202, ten Jiamusi pulsars, and three MeerKAT pulsars. While J0437-4715 provides the primary phase-domain rejection of the ψ=0 null, PSR J1603-7202 contributes complementary bipolar geometric structure from a distinct line of sight at different velocity geometry. The Jiamusi and MeerKAT samples are noise-limited because they contain only 1–2 closure-capable epochs; they provide bounds but do not independently diagnose environmental suppression.

J0437-4715 shows a non-zero Phase Closure signal. Because the Rayleigh and V-test statistics measure circular concentration against uniformity (and are rotation-invariant with respect to the mean angle), circular concentration and zero-mean rejection are reported separately: the unweighted circular mean is ψ=1.120 rad (95% bootstrap CI [+0.988,+1.254] rad excluding zero at 5.7σ–12.7σ across 15–240-epoch contiguous MJD blocks and 16.8σ i.i.d.; circular concentration Rayleigh p=1.34×10⁻⁴⁴), and the weighted circular mean is ψ̄=0.984±0.046 rad with 95% circular-bootstrap confidence interval [0.737,1.235] rad excluding zero at 5.2σ–6.6σ across 15–240-epoch MJD blocks (7.9σ i.i.d. at effective sample size n_eff=313, SE_boot=0.125 rad; 21.2σ analytical circular SE, Step 059; corroborated in the delay domain by the MJD-ordered 15–240-epoch block-bootstrap noise-subtracted excess at 3.8σ–10.8σ, Steps 052 and 060; circular concentration R̄=0.308, Rayleigh p=1.39×10⁻¹³, V-test p=1.02×10⁻⁵ relative to μ_0 = 0). The unweighted statistic is identical in heliocentric and CMB-frame analyses, providing an implementation-invariance check; physical discrimination comes from the non-zero mean closure offset and its null controls.

PSR J1603-7202 has a 73.8° proper-motion separation from J0437-4715 and exhibits a frame-independent bipolar decomposition of the morphology expected for high-dispersion sightlines; its circular Phase Closure is noise-limited (R̄ = 0.019, Rayleigh p = 0.926), so the decomposition is a directional consistency check rather than demonstrated parity structure. Delay-domain unsigned amplitude estimators (M_cl) and their folded-normal Rice noise-floor bounds are documented in Appendix B.

Multiple independent checks support the phase-domain result. Phase-scramble and pre-alignment controls pass, unweighted ψ is strictly frame-invariant, signed-delay cancellation behaves as expected for a bipolar signal, and rigorous synthetic noise tests yielded zero false positives in 100 synthetic null trials. In the 5.74-day binary orbital channel, while the naive signed-delay mean is attenuated by within-epoch bipolar cancellation (1.11 ± 0.79 ns, p = 0.372, 2 df), evaluating the identical REML mixed-effects model on the unsigned delay magnitude M_cl yields A = 5.71 ± 1.10 ns (LR = 26.57, p = 1.70×10⁻⁶; 6.42 ± 1.19 ns, LR = 28.70, p = 5.86×10⁻⁷ on the 1,093 independent epochs) and on the paired noise-subtracted excess M_cl - E[M_cl] halves the inter-epoch random scatter (σ_α: 20.95 → 10.60 ns) to resolve a statistically significant modulation of A = 1.14 ± 0.45 ns (LR = 6.32, p = 0.0425, Step 046b), corroborated in the phase domain by the 1-parameter Thiele–Innes binary reflex velocity projection onto the 128° screen axis (2Δln L = 5.70, 1 df p = 0.0170, ΔAIC = -3.70, Step 051). Multi-pulsar scaling, chromaticity, cross-telescope environmental bounds, and orbital structure provide multiscale consistency checks on the TEP framework.

The observation rejects both scalar-factorizable and non-factorizable asymmetric sub-image-blended thin-screen nulls. Unlike spatial VLBI visibility closure (which samples a single sky image at u₁₂+u₂₃+u₃₁=0), secondary-spectrum cross-term closure isolates localized image pairs (τ_b-τ_a, f_D,b-f_D,a); even when M_sub=5 asymmetric anisotropic sub-images with refractive phase wedges blend non-factorizably inside the 3×3 pixel patch, the Hermitian overlap kernel centers the closure distribution at ψ̄_sim=+0.015 rad (1.38σ from zero, Step 061). Across the full 4.45× wideband Parkes DR2 frequency span from 50 cm (710 MHz, 216 epochs), 20 cm (1,376 MHz, 717 epochs, Z=193.4), to 10 cm (3,165 MHz, 458 epochs, Z=189.9), the 20-cm/10-cm phase-delay ratio is 2.441 ± 0.042 (50-cm/10-cm: 5.020 ± 0.118), matching phase-fixed holonomy (ν₁₀/ν₂₀=2.299, ν₁₀/ν₅₀=4.455) while excluding cold-plasma dispersive scaling (∝ν⁻² ⟹ 5.29 and 19.85) at >65σ and refractive wedge scaling (∝ν⁻⁴ ⟹ 27.9 and 394), with the 10-cm paired delay excess (+8.70±0.70 ns, 12.4σ) exceeding 20 cm (+4.57±0.24 ns, 19.3σ; Step 061). Within the TEP measurement model, the non-zero Phase Closure is a synchronization-holonomy candidate sourced by the non-exact disformal sector: on the measured screen-triangle geometry (Step 057), the canonical disformal connection yields ≈ 1.5×10⁻¹² s as a ceiling at order-unity drift inhomogeneity, with the B₀=+1 unit benchmark (1.3% of the narrowband-equivalent H_implied≈ 1.13×10⁻¹⁰ s on the median leg, 5.0% on the 95th-percentile leg) and reaches 1.14×10⁻¹⁰ s (100.5% on the median leg; B₀^req=20.1 on the p₉₅ leg) at the corpus's GW170817 galactic-profile envelope B₀≲ 77.7 (Paper 0, Step 50; while terrestrial optical-resonator bounds constrain B(φ)Σ² at Earth's screened surface where 𝒮_Σ² ≪ 1, decoupled from the unscreened interstellar B(φ)Σ u̇ loop integral, Paper 0 §10.1), while remaining within the delay-domain bound (≲ 0.46 ns).
