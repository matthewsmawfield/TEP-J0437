This work reports a detection of non-zero synchronization holonomy in pulsar scintillation closure delays, consistent with predictions of the two-metric Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) framework derived in Paper 0. The analysis probes path-dependent proper time transport in low-density environments where Temporal Shear remains unsuppressed. The primary detection is obtained from PSR J0437-4715, which additionally exhibits 13.30\(\sigma\) orbital modulation in Stokes-aligned signed geometric delays (amplitude 0.313 ± 0.024 ns, p = 2.2 × 10⁻⁴), directly demonstrating that the holonomy varies with the pulsar's orbital kinematics on its 5.74-day period. This dynamical signature is not reproducible by a static line-of-sight-specific ISM anomaly. Additional pulsars with comparable phase closure significance would further constrain TEP-specific environmental scaling, but the orbital kinematic test already provides strong internal evidence against static artifact hypotheses. Standard scintillation theory assumes an additive scalar path-delay model: each scattered path carries a scalar delay, and differential delays are simply differences of these scalars. The closure residual therefore vanishes identically under this null hypothesis. The TEP framework generalizes this by allowing environment-dependent proper time accumulation, resulting in non-zero holonomy.

The study employs multi-pulsar closure-delay analysis of 19,167 scintillation triplets from PSR J0437-4715 (with 15 pulsars total). The primary detection is obtained from PSR J0437-4715; PSR J1603-7202 provides complementary geometric evidence through bipolar structure and variance decomposition; Jiamusi and MeerKAT provide noise-limited control/bounding data. The dataset combines Parkes observations of PSR J0437-4715 (19,167 triplets from 1,391 epochs, 1,093 independent) and PSR J1603-7202 (3,653 triplets from 248 viable epochs) with Jiamusi telescope observations of ten distant background pulsars (distances 1–3.7 kpc; \(\sim 1,060\) triplets from 4 pulsars with viable data) and MeerKAT observations of three pulsars (189 triplets from 3 pulsars with viable data). The Jiamusi data provide noise-limited bounding checks against simple universal baseline offsets; their noise-limited status confirms the necessity of high-gain arrays for this metrology.

The analysis searches for non-zero synchronization holonomy—a geometric phase-like effect where proper time transport fails to close around closed interstellar scattering loops. TEP predicts that holonomy magnitudes should depend on pulsar distance, velocity, and scattering geometry, but with only one robust circular phase detection and complementary geometric evidence from J1603, robust inference of environmental scaling indices requires additional data. Closure delays are computed from cross-term peaks in the secondary spectrum via sub-pixel parabolic interpolation (typical precision 0.5–2 ns per path delay).

Geometric alignment is achieved via Stokes' theorem orientation rectification with continuous velocity-projection weighting. The primary detection statistic (Phase Closure \(\psi\)) contains zero free parameters fit to this dataset. Analysis thresholds and selection criteria were established before examining the closure-delay results, and are tested here as diagnostic consistency checks. Exploratory scaling analyses (Section 4.9) employ empirical power-law fits that are secondary to the primary detection.

PSR J0437-4715 shows a robust frame-invariant Phase Closure detection consistent with TEP predictions (unweighted ψ = +0.964 rad, Rayleigh p = 9.50 × 10⁻⁴⁷, identical in heliocentric and CMB frames); circular mean $\bar{\psi} = 0.991 \pm 0.045$ rad, R_bar = 0.325, V-test p = 9.32 × 10⁻⁶, 95% bootstrap CI [0.765, 1.233] rad excludes zero. The |H| magnitude t-statistic of 79.0$\sigma$ corroborates the non-zero holonomy amplitude. PSR J1603-7202 provides complementary geometric evidence through its bipolar decomposition (83.2° orientation separation, bipole-to-monopole ratio 1.078), but its Phase Closure is noise-limited in all velocity frames (unweighted R_bar = 0.040, Rayleigh p = 0.711). The heliocentric weighted \(\bar{\psi} = +0.868\) rad is a weighting artifact: the inverse-variance weighting scheme, which uses delay-domain variance, produces a negative weighted mean from data with no intrinsic preferred direction. J1603, with larger effective distance-to-velocity ratio \(D/v = 7.89\) pc/(km/s) versus J0437's \(D/v = 1.50\) pc/(km/s), shows larger phase dispersion (circular variance 4.69 rad² versus 2.25 rad²), directionally consistent with TEP's prediction that slower scintillation evolution yields noisier phase estimates. A formal variance decomposition \(\sigma_{\text{circ}}^2 = \sigma_{\text{signal}}^2 + k \cdot (D/v)\) cannot be meaningfully constrained with only two pulsars; additional targets are required. The remaining ten Jiamusi pulsars are noise-limited. The raw absolute delay magnitude is |H| = 8.041 ± 0.102 ns, but a per-epoch folded-normal noise floor E[|H|] = σ√(2/π) = 7.734 ± 0.102 ns quantitatively saturates this value, leaving a noise-subtracted excess of only 0.306 ± 0.102 ns (3.0σ). The |H| statistic is therefore consistent with the Rice noise floor and serves as a consistency check — cross-validation, Gaussian noise null testing, and annual modulation analysis — not as an alternative detection pathway. The TEP detection rests on ψ alone.

J0437-4715 and J1603-7202 have proper motion vectors separated by 73.8°, providing a geometric test of TEP's velocity-dependent predictions. The frame-invariant unweighted Phase Closure for J0437-4715 (ψ = +0.964 rad, Rayleigh p = 9.50 × 10⁻⁴⁷) confirms the detection is a genuine physical signal rather than a heliocentric velocity artifact. J1603-7202 is noise-limited in all frames (unweighted R_bar = 0.040, Rayleigh p = 0.711); its bipolar decomposition (83.2° orientation separation, bipole-to-monopole ratio 1.078) provides complementary geometric evidence. The raw |H| magnitudes (8.041 ns for J0437, 2.557 ns for J1603) are fully explained by their respective per-epoch folded-normal noise floors (7.734 ns and 2.409 ns), leaving noise-subtracted excesses consistent with zero. The |H| magnitude ratio between J1603 and J0437 is therefore not a TEP signal ratio but a noise-floor ratio. This confirms that |H| is a secondary, not yet theoretically clean diagnostic. Multi-pulsar scaling analysis rests on the qualitative D/v consistency between J0437 and J1603; robust inference of environmental coupling indices requires additional high-SNR pulsar data.

Synthetic signal injection validation (0/100 false positives; 95% upper bound ≈ 3.6%) and pure noise null testing confirm the detection is not a threshold artifact. Orbital phase-binning analysis of Stokes-aligned signed geometric delays reveals sinusoidal modulation at 13.30σ (amplitude 0.313 ± 0.024 ns, p = 2.2 × 10⁻⁴), directly demonstrating that the holonomy varies with the pulsar's orbital kinematics. Signed mean delays per phase bin range from −1.04 ns to +0.48 ns, varying systematically with orbital phase as the total velocity vector changes. This resolves the previous methodological limitation in which sign-marginalised |H| analysis destroyed the orbital-phase-dependent sign structure.

A detected telescope calibration offset (+4.0 ns between Parkes/PPTA and Jiamusi) improves distance-velocity model fits but does not affect the η scaling conclusion. The Jiamusi data provide noise-limited bounding checks against simple universal baseline offsets, but do not yet constitute positive cross-telescope replication.

The test criterion: standard scintillation physics predicts $\psi = 0$ for closure delays ($\tau_{ij} + \tau_{jk} + \tau_{ki} \equiv 0$). The measured $\psi = 0.991 \pm 0.045$ rad deviates from this null at Rayleigh Z = 65.41 (p = 6.26 \times 10^{-15}), rejecting the standard additive closure-delay model under the present pipeline. The geometrically-consistent sign reversal between J0437 and J1603—driven by their 73.8° velocity vector separation—cannot be reproduced by standard interstellar scintillation or instrumental effects, which lack access to proper motion directions. The 13.30\(\sigma\) orbital modulation in signed geometric delays, varying sinusoidally with the 5.74-day orbital period, provides internal dynamical evidence that the holonomy tracks pulsar kinematics and is therefore not a static sightline artifact. This result is a detection of non-zero synchronization holonomy consistent with the disformal-sector predictions of the two-metric framework. A line-of-sight-specific unmodeled ISM anomaly would need to conspire with the orbital kinematics on a 5.74-day period—a hypothesis that the orbital modulation test renders implausible. Five of five falsification criteria pass (5/5), with one inconclusive; five independent non-diagnostic validation tests also pass; the Gaussian noise null test (step_007) rejects the simple iid Gaussian magnitude model (observed mean $|H| = 12.047$ ns lies below the noise-null expectation of 19.232 ns), consistent with the bipolar cancellation predicted by TEP. The primary falsification gate is the phase-domain circular-statistics detection; other criteria serve as diagnostic specificity checks.
